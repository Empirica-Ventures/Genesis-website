SAMPLE PROJECT · STUDENT BOOKLET

The Formula

Design, make, and launch Pakistan's next great drink.

GROUP CONSTITUTION4-5 Students in a group
SUBJECTSScience, Mathematics, English, and Islamiyat
LESSONS10-12 Lessons
DRIVING QUESTIONMix it right, measure it fair: How can we create a healthy drink everyone can trust?
Mission 01 Taste 02 Science 03 Design 04 Calculate 05 Develop 06 Launch 07 Reflect
YOUR MISSION

Food scientists design every drink you have ever tasted, the concentration, the ratios, the ingredients. Now it is your group's turn.

You will design a formula for a new drink using real Pakistani ingredients. You will mix it, test it, label it, and share what you made with an audience that has real questions. Make a group of 4-5 students. Every group makes something different. At the end, your class runs an awareness campaign about honest food labelling, and the audience includes people from outside your classroom.

Pakistan has a problem. Walk into any shop and pick up a juice carton. It will say "30% fruit juice" or "natural flavour" or "no added sugar." But can you trust it? Food safety authorities in Pakistan regularly find that drink labels are inaccurate, percentages are overstated, artificial dyes are unlisted, and sugar content is hidden. Most people who buy these drinks have no idea how to check.

By the end of this project, you will. But first, before you read a single word about mixtures, you are going to taste three drinks and start figuring out what is actually going on.

What You Will Walk Away With

01A real drink you designed and made from scratch, in a bottle, with your original label on it.
02The experience of standing in front of a real audience, answering real questions, and communicating science in plain words that anyone can understand.
03The ability to explain every single ingredient in your formula using Science and Mathematics.
04Your own data, percentages, observations, results, that tells the story of how you built it.

STAGE 04

Calculate & Plan

A good formula is precise. Food scientists measure everything. Now you calculate your ratios, work out your percentages, and design your label. The mathematics you are using here expresses one quantity as a percentage of another, simplifying ratios, writing continued ratios, and solving real-world word problems involving ratio and rate. These are the calculations your drink actually requires.

Your group will start by making 250 ml of your drink: enough for testing, tasting, and the pulp test. If your formula works well, you may scale up to a maximum of 1000 ml for the campaign. Decide your exact starting amounts for each ingredient and fill in the table.

Before filling this in: Pick up a juice or drink packet from home. Find the nutrition label on the back. Notice how each ingredient is listed as a percentage. Now ask yourself: does this look accurate? Could you verify it? That is exactly what you are about to do for your own drink, and the answer should always be: yes, anyone can check it.

Now Work with Your Numbers

You have already calculated percentages in the table above. Now use those same numbers to go deeper, one idea at a time.

PART A

Ratio (comparing two ingredients)

A ratio compares two amounts. If you used 200 ml of juice and 100 ml of water, the ratio of juice to water is 200 : 100. You can simplify this by dividing both numbers by the same value: 200 ÷ 100 = 2, and 100 ÷ 100 = 1. So the simplified ratio is 2 : 1.

Now do the same with your two main ingredients:
My two main ingredients: ______ and ______
Amounts used: ______ ml and ______ ml
Ratio (not simplified): ______ : ______
Simplified ratio: ______ : ______ (Both divided by: ______)

PART B

Continued ratio (three ingredients)

A continued ratio compares three or more things at once. If your drink has juice : water : honey in amounts 150 ml : 100 ml : 50 ml, the continued ratio is 150 : 100 : 50. Divide all three by 50, you get 3 : 2 : 1.

Now do this for your drink's three main ingredients:

Ingredient B: ______ ml
Ingredient C: ______ ml
Continued ratio (not simplified): ____ : ____ : ____
Simplified continued ratio: ____ : ____ : ____ (Divide all by: ____)

PART C

Proportion (scaling your formula)

Proportion means keeping the same ratio but changing the total amount. If your drink is 300 ml and has juice: water in ratio 2: 1, then juice = 200 ml and water = 100 ml. If you want to make 600 ml (double), you multiply both by 2, juice = 400 ml, water = 200 ml. The ratio stays the same, the amounts grow.

PART D

Unit Conversion (ml and L)

When you measure your drink in ml, you are already using the unit of capacity. But food labels and drink cartons also use litres (L). The conversion is simple: 1 L = 1000 ml. So to go from ml to L, divide by 1000. To go from L to ml, multiply by 1000.

Example: 500 ml ÷ 1000 = 0.50 L. Similarly, 0.75 L × 1000 = 750 ml
Now use your own drink's numbers:

THE MORAL PULSE

Before your campaign, read this carefully.

One day, the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ walked through a marketplace and passed a man selling grain. Prophet Muhammad ﷺ put his hand into the pile and found the grain underneath was wet, the seller had hidden damp grain beneath a dry surface to make it look better than it was.

The Prophet ﷺ said:

مَنْ غَشَّنَا فَلَيْسَ مِنَّا

"He who deceives is not of us."

HADITH 101 | SAHIH MUSLIM

This was not just about grain. It was about the standard of Amanah, trustworthiness, that Islam requires in every transaction. Your label is a transaction. When someone reads your label, they are trusting that what is written is what is inside. The percentage you calculated, the ingredients you listed, that is your word.

Think about this:

1.Looking at your final label, is everything on it exactly what your test showed?
2.If a company lists 'natural flavour' on a label but uses artificial dye, what has it done?
3.The filtration test you ran in Stage 2 is the same kind of test food scientists use, separating what is hidden from what is visible. You now have the knowledge to ask: what is actually in this product?
4.If your drink did not turn out as healthy as you hoped, would you still write the truth on the label? Why?
5.Think of one product you or your family buys regularly: chips, juice, and biscuits. Do you think what is written on the label is exactly what is inside?
6.After doing this project, will you read food labels differently? What will you look for?

STAGE 07

Reflect

Answer honestly. Not what sounds right, what is actually true for you.

1.In Step 1 you tasted three cups without knowing anything about mixtures. Now you can describe exactly what each cup was, chemically. Write the scientific description of Cup B and Cup C using all the vocabulary you now know.
2.Look at your final formula (Table 7). Your drink has a percentage for each ingredient. If you were selling this drink and wanted to add 100 ml of extra water to your drink without changing anything else, what would happen to the concentration? What would change, and what would stay the same?
